<Accordions>
<Accordion title="How many pages can one source hold?">

The dashboard form asks for a page limit and defaults to 50 for a first
crawl; the ceiling is 10,000 per source. A large site is better added as
several sources with different start URLs, which also lets you sync the part
that changed.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Why did the crawl find fewer pages than the site has?">

The crawler stops at `maxPages`, skips paths that `robots.txt` disallows for
`User-agent: *`, follows a sitemap index one level deep, and never leaves the
start URL's origin. Pages served only after JavaScript runs are fetched as
their initial HTML. [Website](/knowledge/website#how-it-finds-pages) lists the
rules.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Can I crawl a site behind a login?">

No. The crawler sends no credentials. Documentation behind authentication is
best exported to a public or internal static site the worker can reach.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Does Sync re-embed everything?">

No. Each page's content is hashed; only pages whose hash changed, and new
pages, are re-chunked and re-embedded. A sync of an unchanged site costs a
crawl and no model calls.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="What are the flagged chunks on a source row?">

Chunks that matched one of four injection heuristics at ingest: instruction
overrides, chat role markers, text addressed to a model, or system-prompt
probes. They are counted for you to look at and still answer; nothing is
dropped silently. [Re-indexing](/knowledge/re-indexing#injection-flagging)
describes the checks.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="A page and a written answer disagree. Which wins?">

Whichever matches the question better. Written answers are indexed like pages
and get no boost, so a page that phrases the question the way the visitor did
can outrank the answer written for it. [Written answers](/knowledge/written-answers#how-it-is-indexed-and-ranked)
explains the ranking and when to fix the page instead.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Why did the widget ask a question back instead of answering?">

Because `DESKBELL_CLARIFY` is on and the pages retrieved for that question
answer it in more than one way — typically a question with a pronoun and no
earlier turn to resolve it against, or a term two products or versions share.
The options are titles of pages that were retrieved, and the visitor's pick
becomes the next question.

If it happens on questions that have one obvious reading, the pages involved
probably share a title or an opening paragraph.
[Clarifying questions](/knowledge/clarifying-questions) has the rule it
follows, what it costs and how to measure it.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Why does an answer end with 'I couldn't find … in the documentation'?">

Because the answer named a feature, setting, button or page, in bold, that the
check could not find in the pages retrieved for the question or anywhere in
the index. The sentence is the assistant admitting it; the message carries an
**Unsupported name** badge on the Conversations page and the names land on the
Gaps page. If the name does exist, your pages write it differently: a plural,
a hyphen, a range. [Answer verification](/knowledge/answer-verification) says
what is matched and how to measure the false alarms; the `unsupportedNote` copy
key rewords the sentence, and `DESKBELL_VERIFY=off` turns the check off.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Where do the vectors live?">

In Postgres, in the `chunks` table, through pgvector. With
`DESKBELL_VECTOR_STORE=weaviate` they live in a Weaviate collection instead and
the row records which store holds each one. [Vector store](/deploy/vector-store)
covers both.

</Accordion>
</Accordions>
