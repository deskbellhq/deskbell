---

Some questions are under-specified: "how do I configure it?" when three pages
each configure something different. With `DESKBELL_CLARIFY=on` the assistant
asks which one the visitor meant and offers those pages as options, instead of
answering for whichever reading the model picked. The setting is off by
default, and it is instance-wide rather than per project.

## Turning it on

```bash title=".env"
DESKBELL_CLARIFY=on
```

Restart the web application. Nothing else changes: retrieval, citations and
refusals behave exactly as before, and with the setting off the instruction the
model reads is identical to the one it read before this existed.

<Callout type="warn">
It ships off because it costs accuracy. A model given permission to ask will
sometimes ask about a question that had one obvious reading, and an exchange
that could have been one answer becomes two turns. Measure it on your own
corpus before turning it on for visitors; [What it costs](#what-it-costs) says
how.
</Callout>

## When it asks

Only when the retrieved pages actually disagree about what the question means,
and nothing earlier in the conversation settles it. The model is told to answer
and state its assumption when one reading is the usual one, and to ask only
when the readings lead to different answers.

What it offers is checked against what retrieval returned. Every option has to
be the title of a page that was retrieved for that question; an option naming
anything else is dropped, and if fewer than two survive, the turn becomes a
`no grounding` refusal instead. So the widget never offers a page your corpus
does not hold, and never invents a distinction out of the model's memory.

Two to four options, and **one clarifying question per conversation**. After it
has asked once, the instruction changes: answer the most likely reading and say
what you assumed. A second question in the same thread becomes a refusal.

The visitor's pick is an ordinary next message. It goes back through the same
pipeline as any follow-up, so the question planner resolves it against the
question that was asked and retrieval searches the resolved wording.

## What the visitor sees

The question, with the options as buttons under it. Tapping one sends it as the
next message and the answer follows, cited as any other answer. The buttons
belong to the most recent message; reopening the thread later shows the
question and its options as text.

A clarifying turn can be voted on like an answer. A thumbs-down on one is
recorded and reaches the [Gaps](/operate/gaps) page.

Building your own interface instead of using the widget: the `clarify` event
carries the question and the options over the [HTTP API](/developer/api) and
the [SDK](/developer/sdk), and sending an option back as the next message is
the whole protocol. In the widget, the host page hears `deskbell:clarify`
instead of `deskbell:answer`, and the buttons are styled through the
`clarify-options` and `clarify-option` parts. [JavaScript API](/widget/javascript-api)
covers both.

## What it costs

A clarifying question is a turn the visitor did not ask for. On questions that
were genuinely ambiguous it replaces a guess; on questions that were not, it
replaces an answer that was already correct, and the second kind is the one to
watch.

The [eval harness](/operate/evaluation) reports both sides. **Clarify rate** is
the share of answerable questions that were asked about instead of answered,
which is the cost. **Clarification** is the share of deliberately ambiguous
questions that were asked about with the right options offered, which is the
benefit. They are reported separately, and never averaged, for the same reason
factuality and uncertainty are: asking about everything would make one of them
perfect and the other unbearable.

Run your own question set with the setting off, then on, and compare. A set
with no ambiguous cases in it measures only the cost, so add cases of the kind
before reading the result as a verdict.

## Where it shows up

A clarifying turn is stored as part of the conversation and marked **Asked to
clarify** in the transcript on the Conversations page.

It is **not** counted as a gap. It is not a refusal, and the weak-answer floor
is a property of an answer, so a question asked back neither flags a row nor
moves the weak count. What follows it does count: if the answer after the pick
is refused or poorly grounded, that lands on the Gaps page as it would have
anyway.

## Fixing the content instead

A fork that keeps coming back is usually a property of your documentation
rather than of the visitor's question. Two pages with near-identical titles,
or one page whose opening paragraph repeats another's, look like two readings
of the same question to retrieval and to the model.

When you see the same options offered again and again, the durable fix is on
the content side: give the pages titles that say what distinguishes them, merge
the ones that overlap, or [write an answer](/knowledge/written-answers) that
handles the ambiguous phrasing directly. [Writing for retrieval](/knowledge/website)
covers the same ground for refusals.

## Limits

- One clarifying question per conversation.
- Options are page titles, always drawn from the pages retrieved for that
  question. There is no way to offer an option of your own wording.
- The setting is instance-wide. A deployment cannot turn it on for one project
  and off for another.
- The buttons are live on the most recent message only. A replayed thread shows
  the question and options as text.
