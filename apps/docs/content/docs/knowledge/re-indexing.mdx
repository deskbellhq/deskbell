---
title: Re-indexing
description: Changing the embedding model means re-embedding every chunk. The job is resumable, refuses a dimension mismatch before writing anything, and pauses ingestion while it runs.
updated: 2026-08-25
---

Every chunk is stored with the version of the embedding model that produced it.
When you change `DESKBELL_EMBEDDING_MODEL`, the chunks already in the database
were embedded by a different model, and vectors from two models are not
comparable. Re-indexing brings them all onto the current one.

## When you need it

A banner appears on the Sources page when the stored embedding versions diverge
from the configured model. That is the signal.

You do not need it after adding a source. New chunks are stamped with the
organisation's current generation as they are written, so they join the existing
set already correct.

## What the job does

It re-embeds every chunk whose version is below the target, and updates each row
as it goes.

**It is resumable.** The work is selected by predicate rather than by a fixed
list, so completed rows drop out of scope. If the job stops halfway, running it
again continues from where it stopped rather than starting over. Running it when
nothing is stale touches zero rows.

**It refuses a dimension mismatch before writing anything.** The column is
`vector(1536)`. The job embeds a single probe string first and compares the
result's length to the column. If they disagree it stops, and the error names
the migration you need. Nothing is written and nothing is left half-converted.

That matters because the alternative is worse than an error: a partial
conversion leaves a corpus where some vectors are comparable and some are not,
and retrieval quietly degrades rather than failing.

<Callout type="warn">
A full re-index pauses ingestion. The job queue runs serially across the whole
worker, so while re-indexing runs, crawls and new-document embedding wait. On a
large corpus, plan for that.
</Callout>

## Changing embedding model

1. Check the dimension of the new model. `text-embedding-3-small` is 1536.
   `nomic-embed-text` is 768.
2. If it differs from 1536, migrate the column first. The re-index job will
   refuse until you do.
3. Set `DESKBELL_EMBEDDING_MODEL` and restart.
4. Run the re-index from the banner on the Sources page.
5. Wait. Every chunk is sent to the provider again, so this costs whatever
   embedding your whole corpus costs.

Because of step 5, this is a decision worth making before you index a large
corpus rather than after.

## Injection flagging

At ingest, each chunk is checked against four deterministic heuristics:

- **Instruction override.** Text telling a reader to ignore, disregard or
  discard previous instructions, or announcing new ones.
- **Role markers.** Chat-transcript role labels embedded in prose. A
  documentation page has no reason to impersonate a conversation.
- **Assistant address.** Imperatives aimed at a model reading the page rather
  than at a person.
- **System-prompt probes.** Text asking for the instructions the assistant was
  given.

Matches are recorded on the chunk and surfaced as a flagged count per source in
the dashboard.

The checks are deterministic and involve no model call, because this runs on
every chunk at ingest scale and a model call there would multiply the cost of
ingestion.

**Flagged chunks still answer.** Flagging is visibility rather than filtering. A
false positive costs you a look at your own documentation; a silent drop costs a
customer part of their documentation with nothing to say why. On a real crawled
corpus of 97 chunks the heuristics produced no false positives, and ordinary
documentation imperatives such as "click Save" or "run the installer" are
specifically tested to stay quiet.

What the engine should do with a flag beyond showing it is deliberately
undecided until there is data to decide it on.
