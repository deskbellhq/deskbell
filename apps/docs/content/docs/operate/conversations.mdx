**Conversations** lists every thread in the project, newest activity first,
and opens each as a transcript with the sources every answer cited. It is the
place to read a thread a visitor complained about, or to see what a refusal was
refusing. The newest thread of the current view is open when the page loads.

![The Conversations page: the inbox on the left, a thread with a downvoted answer and its cited pages in the middle, the details panel on the right](/images/dashboard-conversations.png)

## The inbox

Each row answers three questions in three places. A disc at the left says
how the thread's answers went: filled green for **certain**, filled grey
for **uncertain** (a refusal, an answer whose best retrieved match scored
under the weak floor, or an answer that named something your content does
not contain, the rule the [Overview](/operate#what-uncertain-means)
explains), hollow for a thread nothing has answered yet. The text is the
title, a preview of the last message and when it was last active. Small
glyphs at the right say why a person should read it: a visitor marked an
answer down, asked for a person, or pushed back on an answer (the
[signals](/operate/usage#intent-and-signals) the worker writes). Nothing on a
row is coloured except the disc, so colour means one thing.

Two views sit above the list with their counts for the period: **All**, and
**Needs attention**, which is every thread the disc or a glyph marks:
uncertain, marked down, asking for a person, or pushing back. The view is
part of the address (`/conversations?view=attention`), so it can be
bookmarked or shared, and it stays selected while you move between threads.
The Overview's Conversations card links here with the same number.

**+ Filter** narrows either view by one property at a time: the outcome
(certain, uncertain, unanswered), feedback (marked down or up), an intent, a
signal, one of your topics, or any context key your pages attach, with the
ids seen under it named the way your page named them. Each narrowing is a
chip above the list with a way back, and each is part of the address
(`?outcome=uncertain`, `?feedback=down`, `?intent=request`,
`?signal=wants_human`, `?topic=billing`, `?ctx.account=acc_42`). The
[Usage](/operate/usage) page's numbers open here with the matching chip; a
signed-in visitor arrives the same way (`?user=…`), since there is no list
of visitors to pick from. Links written with the older `?filter=down` and
`?filter=uncertain` still open the same threads.

A period control sits beside the count: **All time**, **Today**, **Last 7
days**, **Last 30 days**, **Last 90 days**, or a custom range of two dates.
Days are UTC calendar days, the same buckets the Overview chart draws, and a
relative period is the last so many days ending today. A preset is part of
the address too (`/conversations?period=7d`), and a custom range is its two
days (`/conversations?from=2026-09-01&to=2026-09-23`). Clicking a column of
the Overview chart opens the page on that one day. The two view counts are
for the period; the list shows up to two hundred of its most recently
active threads.

A title is the first question trimmed to eighty characters, or one short
chat-model call per conversation when `DESKBELL_THREAD_TITLES=model`. A visitor
can rename their own threads from the widget's sidebar; the dashboard shows the
name they chose.

## The thread

Under the title, one line sums the thread up in the same three slots as the
inbox row: the status badge, then **About** with the thread's topics and
intents as plain pills, then **Needs** with what asks for a person: a
human, pushed back, marked down. Hovering a pill shows the rule. The
transcript alternates the visitor's messages and the assistant's answers,
rendered with the same Markdown the widget shows. Beside each question, when
labelling is on, a badge names its intent and each signal it carries,
frustration included; the lists and the switch are explained under
[Usage](/operate/usage#intent-and-signals). Under each answer:

- **Sources.** A pill per cited page, linking to it. A cited written answer
  shows a pen glyph and no link.
- **A refusal, named.** The assistant refused, and the badge says why:
  **Nothing retrieved** when no page matched at all, **No grounded answer**
  when pages matched but did not support an answer, **Off topic**,
  **Blocked by guardrail**, or **No access to visitor data** when the
  visitor asked about their own account and read the reply from
  **Settings › Visitor data** ([Questions about the visitor's own data](/knowledge/own-data-questions)).
  Hovering the badge shows how many chunks were retrieved and the best
  score. The first two are what the [Gaps](/operate/gaps) page clusters.
- **Weak grounding.** The answer was given, but from a best match under the
  weak floor. The same hover shows the retrieval behind it.
- **Unsupported name.** The answer named a feature, setting, button or page
  that exists nowhere in your content; the hover lists the names. The visitor
  saw a sentence under the answer saying so
  ([Answer verification](/knowledge/answer-verification)); never recorded
  with `DESKBELL_VERIFY=off`.
- **Asked to clarify.** The docs answer the question in more than one way and
  the visitor was asked which they meant.
- **The footer line.** When the answer was written, how long the model took,
  and which model wrote it. When the answer belongs to a gap cluster, an
  **Open in Gaps** link goes to that cluster.
- **Feedback.** A thumbs-up or thumbs-down, and for a thumbs-down the reason
  the visitor picked and any comment they typed. The built-in reasons read as
  sentences; a reason the page configured itself
  ([feedback reasons](/widget/configuration/behaviour#feedback-reasons)) shows
  its id, since only the page knows its label.

The badge at the top of the thread summarises it: **Certain**, **Uncertain**
with a note such as `1 of 4 refused · 1 weak` or `1 unsupported`, or
**Unanswered** when the thread has no assistant message.

## Details

The panel beside the transcript shows the thread's id, when it started, the
session it belongs to, and the pages cited across the whole thread. A thread
the visitor deleted from the widget stays readable here and says when it was
deleted.

When the project verifies [visitor identity](/widget/identity), the panel
shows the signed-in visitor by name, email or subject, and the thread belongs
to that visitor across browsers. When the page passes
[context](/widget/configuration/behaviour#where-a-conversation-starts), each key
appears as a row: which account, which site, which whatever your page named,
with the id and the display name the page supplied.

## What is stored

A conversation holds its messages, each question's topic, intent and
signals, each answer's citations, the retrieval signal behind it (how many
chunks matched and the best score), the refusal reason, feedback, and the
context the page supplied when the thread started.
Visitor IP addresses are never stored; rate limiting keeps only an HMAC hash.
[Security](/manage/security#privacy-of-visitor-addresses) covers what leaves
the instance, which is nothing except the calls to your model provider.

Conversations are kept until the project's retention window removes them.
[Data retention](/manage/data-retention) sets the window in days, counted
from the last message; the hourly job removes older conversations with
everything listed above.
