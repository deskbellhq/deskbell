The Gaps page in the dashboard is the list of questions your content could
not answer. Each row is a need, not a phrasing: "how do I set up SSO", "sso
setup" and "configure single sign-on" are one row with a count of three. Rows
carry what the assistant found or cited, with a cited
[written answer](/knowledge/written-answers) marked as one, how often the need
came up and whether it is rising, and, on request, a written finding and
recommendation. **Details** opens a drawer with all of it and the actions for
the row. Every row can become an eval case, and the whole view downloads as a
spreadsheet or a dataset.

![The Gaps page: three open clusters, one refused, one weakly grounded and one marked down, each with the pages that were found or cited](/images/dashboard-gaps.png)

## What counts as a gap

Four signals make an answer a gap. Two come from the visitor and two from the
engine.

| Signal | What happened |
|---|---|
| Refused | The assistant declined to answer. `no context` means retrieval returned nothing relevant; `no grounding` means it returned passages and the model would not answer from them |
| Marked down | A visitor gave the answer a thumbs-down, with a reason and an optional comment. This one can also [notify a channel](/operate/notifications) as it happens |
| Weak answer | The assistant answered, but its best retrieved passage scored under `DESKBELL_GAPS_WEAK_SCORE` |
| Unsupported name | The assistant answered and named a feature, setting, button or page that exists nowhere in your content. [Answer verification](/knowledge/answer-verification) explains the check; `DESKBELL_VERIFY=off` turns it off |

Answers written before the engine started recording its evidence count only on
the first two signals: a missing score never flags a row, and an answer that
was never checked for names is not an unsupported-name answer.

An unsupported-name row carries the names. The drawer lists them with how
often each was used across the cluster, the CSV export has them in an
`unsupported_terms` column, the task brief quotes them, and the analysis is
told which names the answers invented, so a finding can say "the assistant
keeps offering a *Find and Replace* step that the docs do not have".

A [clarifying question](/knowledge/clarifying-questions) is none of the three.
It is not a refusal, and the weak-answer floor is a property of an answer, so
asking the visitor which reading they meant neither flags a row nor moves the
weak count. A thumbs-down on one still counts, and so does whatever the answer
after the visitor's pick turns out to be.

The row also says when an answer cited everything the engine retrieved because
the model named no source. That is counted and shown, not treated as a gap: on
the shipped chat model it is the common case, and it points at generation
rather than at your content.

## How rows are grouped

A worker job assigns each gap to a cluster. An exact repeat of an earlier
question joins its cluster without any model call. A new wording is embedded
with the configured embedding model and compared with every wording the project
has already seen — not just the phrasing each cluster is labelled with — so a
cluster keeps taking in paraphrases of the questions it already holds. It joins
the cluster of the closest wording when the similarity reaches
`DESKBELL_GAPS_CLUSTER_SIMILARITY`; otherwise it starts a cluster, labelled with
that first wording.

Before a question is embedded, the terms in **Settings → Gaps** are removed from
it. Your own name appears in most of the questions people ask and tells none of
them apart: left in, it pulls unrelated questions together, and two of them can
end up as one row. Stripping it applies to the comparison only — the question
you read keeps every word. By default the term is your workspace's name; the
setting takes any list, and an empty list strips nothing.

The threshold is a property of the embedding model, and both it and the ignored
terms only affect questions asked after the change. To group the questions you
already have the same way, use **Regroup existing questions** in Settings →
Gaps, which rebuilds every cluster in the project from the questions — and drops
the clusters you marked resolved, because rebuilt groups are a different set.

If the embedding provider is unavailable, questions still land in a cluster by
their text and an hourly catch-up job fills in the vectors and anything the
per-message assignment missed. Until the worker has run, the page groups by
exact wording and says so.

## Period, trend and order

The page shows the last 30 days by default; 7 days, 90 days and all time are a
click away and live in the URL, so a view is a link. The totals above the table
compare the period with the equal-length period before it, and each row carries
a trend: **New** when the need was not seen in the previous period, **Up** or
**Down** when its count moved by a quarter or more.

The default order weights every occurrence by its age, halving every seven
days, so a question refused for the first time today sits above a pair of
refusals from three weeks ago while a burst from yesterday still outranks both.
**Most asked** orders by count; **Trending** by the rise against the previous
period.

The page shows **open** clusters by default: it is the list of things to do.
The status control beside the period switches to **Resolved**, the clusters
marked resolved or answered in the period, or **All**, where resolved
clusters sit at the bottom of any order. Each choice shows its count, and the
status is part of the URL like the period and the order. The totals and their
deltas follow the view: on the resolved view they count resolved clusters in
this period against resolved clusters in the one before.

## Resolving a gap

**Mark resolved** records that the content now answers the need. The row
leaves the open view and the Resolved view lists it; a gap closed by a written
answer is listed there as **Answered**. If the question comes up again after
that, the cluster reopens by itself, returns to the open view and shows
**Recurring**: the fix did not hold, or a visitor found a phrasing it does not
cover. Reopen is one click too.

**Write an answer** closes the gap from inside the dashboard. The row's
drawer and its menu open a form with the question filled in; the answer is
saved as a [written answer](/knowledge/written-answers), indexed like a page,
and the cluster is marked **Answered** with a link to it. An answer that fails
to carry the next occurrence reopens the cluster like any other fix, and the
drawer then offers to edit it.

**Draft with AI**, on the answer field, fills it from the cluster's own
material: the questions visitors asked, the passages the assistant
retrieved, and the analysis if it has run. It is deliberately unwilling to
guess. A gap is a question your content could not answer, so the passages
usually do not hold the answer, and a draft that read fluently from them
would be inventing — an invented answer, once indexed, is cited and looks
authoritative. Instead the draft writes what the passages state and leaves
a blank in square brackets wherever a fact is missing, naming what is
needed, and lists those blanks under the field. You fill the blanks rather
than compose the answer. On a cluster whose pages did cover the question,
where retrieval or grounding was what failed, it comes back with no blanks
at all.

A draft never destroys what you had: **Undo draft** restores it. Drafting
spends tokens against the same daily budget as analysis, and is refused
with a visible message when the budget is gone.
`DESKBELL_ANSWER_DRAFTER_MODEL` picks a different model for drafting than
the one answering visitors.

The drawer is part of the URL: `/gaps?gap=<cluster>` opens it, and
`&write=1` opens it on the form, so a gap can be sent as a link and browser
back closes it. Its actions stay in a footer at the bottom of the drawer.

## Analysis

**Analyze** asks the configured chat model to read a cluster, its phrasings and
the passages the assistant had, and write a title, a finding (what visitors
wanted and why the passages did not answer it), a recommendation (what to write
or change, concretely) and up to five draft marking criteria. The result stays
on the cluster; the row shows the title and recommendation, and the drawer
shows all of it, with **Analyze** or **Re-analyze** beside its heading.

Analysis is a deliberate spend. It runs in the worker, counts against the
project's daily token budget, is refused with a visible message when the
budget is spent, and is skipped when nothing about the cluster changed since
the last run. Editors trigger it per cluster or with **Analyze N open**, which
sends the open, unanalysed clusters in the current view, up to twenty per
click. The bar above the table confirms the click at once, shows how many of
the clusters are done while the worker runs, and says when the findings are
ready; a request the worker has not answered in fifteen minutes is treated as
lost and can be sent again. Setting `DESKBELL_GAPS_AUTO_ANALYZE_AT` to a number of
occurrences analyses a cluster once, automatically, when it reaches that many
in 30 days; it is off by default because automatic spend is something you turn
on. `DESKBELL_GAP_ANALYST_MODEL` picks a different model for analysis than the
one answering visitors.

Everything the model reads is treated as material, not instructions, and
everything it writes is shown as plain text.

## From a gap to a coding agent

**Copy prompt for your coding agent**, in the drawer under the analysis and in
the row's menu, puts
the gap on the clipboard as a Markdown brief that a coding agent, a colleague
or an issue can work from without asking anything back: the questions as
visitors typed them, the pages the assistant had with their URLs, the finding
and recommendation, the draft criteria as a checklist of what the content
must state, the steps to make and verify the change with a link back to the
gap, and the eval case. Paste it into an agent session opened on the
documentation checkout and the agent has what it needs to find the page,
change it and say when it is done. Without analysis the brief still carries
the questions, the pages and the steps, and says that analysis has not run.

Everything a visitor typed is quoted in the brief, and the brief says so at
the top, because the agent reading it may have write access to a repository:
a question typed into the widget is material for it to read, not an
instruction for it to follow.

**Prompts for your coding agent** in the Export menu downloads the same brief for
every cluster in the current view as one file, which pairs with **Analyze N
open**: analyse the view, download it, and hand an agent the lot.

## From a gap to a measured fix

**Copy as eval case**, in the row's menu and in the drawer's, puts a case on
the clipboard in the format the
[eval harness](/operate/evaluation) loads: the question, a placeholder marking
criterion you replace with the real one (or the analysis's draft criteria,
marked as suggested), an empty list of relevant pages, and the provenance of
the message it came from. Paste it into your dataset, write the criteria, add
the page that answers it, and the next eval run measures whether the fix
worked.

**Export** downloads the current view. The CSV has one row per cluster with the
counts, trend, dates, downvote reasons, sample phrasings, evidence documents,
the latest conversation and the analysis, for a spreadsheet or an issue
tracker; questions that start with a formula character are escaped. The dataset
is one answerable case per cluster in the harness's own format:

```bash
pnpm evals --dataset ~/Downloads/deskbell-gaps-my-project-30d-2026-09-10.json \
  --project proj_x --retrieval-only
```

It loads as downloaded and grades visibly wrong until someone writes the
criteria, which is the point: a person decides what a correct answer contains.

## Settings

| Variable | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `DESKBELL_GAPS_WEAK_SCORE` | `0.35` | Top retrieval score under which an answered question is a weak answer |
| `DESKBELL_VERIFY` | `on` | Checks the names every answer asserts against your content and flags the ones found nowhere; `off` records every answer as unchecked |
| `DESKBELL_GAPS_CLUSTER_SIMILARITY` | `0.80` | Similarity at which a new wording joins the cluster of the closest wording |
| `DESKBELL_GAPS_AUTO_ANALYZE_AT` | `0` (off) | Occurrences in 30 days at which a cluster without an analysis is analysed automatically |
| `DESKBELL_GAP_ANALYST_MODEL` | The chat model | The model that writes analyses |
| `DESKBELL_GAP_ANALYST_TIMEOUT_MS` | `30000` | Past it an analysis fails and the cluster shows the error |
| `DESKBELL_ANSWER_DRAFTER_MODEL` | The chat model | The model that drafts written answers |
| `DESKBELL_ANSWER_DRAFTER_TIMEOUT_MS` | `30000` | Past it a draft fails and the form says so |

The two thresholds were measured on `openai/text-embedding-3-small`. A
different embedding model moves both scales; the
[configuration reference](/deploy/environment-variables) says how to re-measure.

Terms to ignore are per project and live in the dashboard, not in the
environment: **Settings → Gaps**.

## Limits

- Clusters form incrementally in the order questions arrive. Two worker
  replicas racing on two phrasings of one new need can create two clusters;
  the next re-cluster merges them.
- The similarity threshold was chosen on a small set of pairs. Expect to move
  it once a real project has traffic, and expect a strict threshold to show
  one need as two rows rather than hide a question inside another.
- Analysis is only as good as the passages the engine had. A cluster whose
  retrieval found nothing gets a finding that says so.
- The page is not paginated. A project with thousands of gaps in a period
  will need the period narrowed; the status filter is the other lever, since
  the default view leaves out everything already resolved.
