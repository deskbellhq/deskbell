The Usage page in the dashboard is the count of how much the widget is used,
by whom in the aggregate, about what, and how it went. Four cards, a chart and
up to five tables, all computed from the stored conversations on every read,
so the page is always current.

Every number is a count over **visitor questions** in the period. A
conversation counts when it had a question in the period, a visitor is a
signed-in person or, when anonymous, a browser session, and a returning
visitor is one who asked on more than one day. The bounded periods also show
the change against the equal-length period before.

## Dimensions come from your pages

The widget's `context` attribute attaches identifiers to a conversation when it
starts — which account, which site, which kind of user (see
[Where a conversation starts](/widget/configuration/behaviour#where-a-conversation-starts)).
The Usage page turns every key it finds into a control:

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Split by** | One series per value of the key: a line on each card, a stack in the chart, a column per topic. More than six values keep the largest five and fold the rest into *Other*; conversations without the key are *No key* |
| **Break down by** | One table row per value of the key, with visitors, conversations, questions and when it was first and last seen. Click a row to filter the page to it |
| **Filter** | `key=id` pairs the conversations must carry, shown as chips. The chart and the topic rows carry the first one into the Conversations page |

Nothing in Deskbell knows what a key means. To report on a new dimension,
add a key to the `context` your page sends; the page offers it as soon as a
conversation carries it. A key like `user_type` with values `staff` and
`customer` is how a host separates its own people from its customers without
the dashboard knowing who anyone is.

Every column head on the page explains its rule when you hover or focus
it, and each table opens with one sentence that says who leads and what
moved most, so the numbers need no legend.

## Topics

Under **Settings › Topics** an admin names what visitors ask about: one topic
per line, as `Name: what belongs here`, up to twenty. From then on each
question is labelled with one topic by the chat model as it comes in — off the
visitor's path, within the project's daily token budget — and a question that
fits none of them counts under *Other*. The description is what the model is
told, so write it as a rule: "Exports: feeds, files, formats, schedules".

The Topics table on the Usage page counts questions per topic, drawn as a
bar beside the number, with each topic's share of all questions in the
period, the change against the previous period, conversations, and a column
per series when the page is split. A topic links to the Conversations page filtered to
it. Questions the worker has not labelled yet sit in one row at the bottom;
an hourly job catches up on them.

Renaming a topic changes its key, so the settings page offers **Re-label**: it
clears every label in the project — topic, intent and signals — and labels
every question again against the current list, oldest first, a few hundred
per hour.

## Intent and signals

Topics say what a question was about. The same model call also writes why it
was asked and how it went, from two fixed lists that are the same for every
project, so there is nothing to configure: the switch at the top of
**Settings › Topics**, **Label every question**, is on by default and is the
one setting. Off means no model call per question and no labels of any kind,
topics included.

Every question gets exactly one **intent**:

| Intent | Rule | What it points at |
|---|---|---|
| How-to | Wants to do something: set up, use, change, achieve a result | A doc, or a written answer |
| Troubleshooting | Something is wrong, failing or not as expected | A doc, or a gap when unanswered |
| Lookup | Wants a fact: a limit, a price, a definition, whether something is supported | Often a one-line answer |
| Request | Asks for something the product does not do, or asks whether it can and the answer is no | The product backlog |
| Off topic | Not about the product at all | Nothing |
| Other | Fits none of the above: greetings, tests, fragments | Nothing |

and any number of **signals**, usually none:

| Signal | Rule | What it points at |
|---|---|---|
| Pushback | Repeats or disputes the previous answer: "no, I meant…", "that's wrong", "that didn't work" | Read the thread; a wrong or missing page the retrieval scores cannot see |
| Frustration | Negative tone, urgency or complaint | Read the thread |
| Wants a human | Asks for a person, support, contact details, a ticket or a call | A handoff the widget could not make |

The **Intent** table reads like the Topics table — questions, share, change
against the previous period, conversations, a column per series when split —
and its sentence names the leading intent and the share of requests, the one
intent that is a product signal rather than a documentation one. The
**Signals** table lists the three signals the same way; a question with two
signals counts under both, and the share is of every question in the period,
so the column does not add up to 100. Both link into the Conversations page
narrowed to the label, and the thread view shows each question's intent and
signals as badges beside it ([Conversations](/operate/conversations#the-thread)).

The model reads the question and the visitor's previous question in the
thread, so a follow-up inherits its topic and "no, I meant the API" counts
as pushback against the answer before it. Every rule above is also the
tooltip on the page.

## Visitors

When the project [verifies who its visitors are](/widget/identity), the page
lists every signed-in visitor who asked in the period, most questions first
— by name, else email, else the token's subject, the same label the thread
view shows — with their questions, conversations, days active and last
question. A visitor is a
link to their threads on the Conversations page, narrowed with a chip you can
remove. The first ten show; one button opens the rest. A visitor who asked on
three or more different days of the period is marked a **power user** and the
heading counts them: the rule counts days, not questions, so a habit shows
and one long afternoon does not.

Anonymous visitors have no profile. A browser session is counted in every
number and never listed; the table's heading says how many sessions were
counted beside the names. The breakdown table shows the ids and names your
page attached to the conversation — your accounts, your sites — never a
visitor's own.
